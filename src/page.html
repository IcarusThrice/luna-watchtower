    Restricted addresses

    Since the v2.22.0 upgrade on October 6, 2026, these addresses cannot start transactions. Phoenix Foundation labels most of them as wallets affected by the legacy Terra Station Desktop flaw and the rest as attacker-associated. One attacker-associated entry is a Gate.io deposit address the exploiter used, which does not implicate the exchange. Funds stay at their addresses until governance decides.

    Quantum readiness

    A large enough quantum computer could derive a private key from a public key. No such machine exists yet, and moving a chain to quantum-safe signatures takes years. This page tracks how far Terra has to go.

    Terra's path to quantum-safe signatures

      How close is the hardware

      Physical qubits, on a log scale. Each step right is ten times more.

      The gap between today's machines and the lowest published estimate is roughly 400 to 500 times. Google has set 2029 as the deadline for its own migration, and one 2026 study estimates a chain that starts now finishes around 2029 to 2031.

      Is an address exposed

      An address is a hash of the public key. The key itself is published the first time the wallet signs a transaction. Until then a quantum attacker has nothing to work from.

      Shows whether the public key is on-chain and which signature scheme it uses.
